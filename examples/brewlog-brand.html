01

Identity

The fixed centre of the system: what the product is, in one line, and the four words every later decision is measured against.

Essence

A tasting journal for people who weigh their coffee: log the brew, rate the cup, learn what your good days have in common.

iOS, single-handed, entered mid-pour. Notes are written in the ninety seconds while the water drains, so the interface must accept a dose and a number faster than it asks for prose.

Mood

WarmArtisanalPreciseCalm

Warm and artisanal come from paper, ink and old-style serif: never from a brown wash. Precise and calm come from tabular numbers, hairlines and one accent.

05

Mascot and logo

v1.1 overrides v1 on exactly one point: Brewlog has a mascot. It is not an illustration: it is one more member of the icon family, drawn on the same grid at the same stroke, given a face and a strict set of rules about when it is allowed to move.

Master drawing · calm · 32 × 32 keyline

Who it is
The V60 dripper standing on its legs: the one object every entry in the log passes through. Cone, rim, two legs, a face and the drip. Eight strokes, no more.
Grid
32 × 32 with a 2 px keyline, drawn on the vertical centreline. Scaled from the 24 × 24 icon grid so the two families stay siblings rather than cousins.
Stroke
2.3 px at 32: the same optical ratio as the icons' 1.75 px at 24. Round caps, round joins, no fill, currentColor. Nothing is ever filled in, not even the eyes.
Face
Two 2.2-unit eye strokes and one 3.4-unit mouth, and that is the whole vocabulary. No pupils, no eyebrows, no blush, no cheeks, no arms. The four states re-cut the same two eye strokes: vertical, arched, angled, closed.
Clearance
No stroke edge comes closer than 0.7 units to the cone wall, so the face never welds itself to the body at any size. The drip hangs 0.5 units clear of the spout: it is falling, not attached.
Colour
Inherits text colour like any other icon. The only honey it may carry is the drip, and only while the timer is running: which keeps principle 02 intact.
Precise, warm
It reads warm because the strokes are open and the proportions are generous, not because it is grinning. Nothing squashes, nothing bounces, nothing waves.

Expressions: four states, no more

Calm

Default · idle

The only state that may sit on screen indefinitely. Used in the log header and the tab bar; static, no motion, ever.

Pleased

Event · brew logged

Fires once on save, on the confirmation screen only. Two burst iterations, then it returns to calm and stays there.

Focused

Event · timer running

Held for the length of the drawdown and nothing longer. The drip turns honey: this is the mascot's one licensed accent, and it counts as the screen's single honey element.

Resting

Empty state

Eyes closed, drip removed: the dripper is dry. Used once, above the empty-log sentence; never as a loading indicator.

Behaviour rules

Calm by default, expressive only on events

  1. 01Calm is the resting state. If nothing has happened, the mascot is calm and completely still. A state other than calm without a triggering event is a bug.
  2. 02Expression follows an event, never a mood. Pleased needs a saved brew, focused needs a running timer, resting needs an empty log. No idle personality, no greetings, no tips.
  3. 03Motion arrives in short bursts: 2–3 iterations, 380 ms each, then done. One 180 ms ease in and out per burst, no bounce, no overshoot beyond 4 %.
  4. 04Never an infinite loop. Nothing on the mascot is set to infinite. Principle 06 still holds: the brew timer is the only element allowed to animate continuously, and the mascot is not the timer.
  5. 05Never animated beside text the user is editing. While a tasting-note field has focus the mascot is frozen at calm: and it is never placed inside or adjacent to that field's container.
  6. 06One mascot per screen, and it yields. It is decoration with a job, so it is the first thing dropped when space is short. Under prefers-reduced-motion the state still changes; the burst does not play.

The bed seen from directly above: three pour rings, the honey one marking where the water is now. Abstract enough to avoid the cup cliché, and it survives the 60 px test.

08

Principles & anti-patterns

The dominance ratio, read left to right, is the fastest way to check a screen before it ships.

  • 60 %paper + white surfaces
  • 30 %ink + roast structure
  • 10 %honey: in practice ≈ 3 %
01

60 / 30 / 10, measured in area, not intent. If a screen reads as brown, the roast band has crept past 30 %: pull it back to structure and type.

02

Honey is reserved for three things: a rating fill, the extraction marker, and the running timer. One honey element per screen; if a second appears, one of them is wrong.

03

Honey never becomes text. At 2.7:1 on white it fails as a foreground: links and brand text use --primary-600 at 12.6:1 instead.

04

Numbers belong to the mono, words to the sans, feeling to the serif. A dose set in the body sans is a bug, not a style choice.

05

Copy is precise and second-person, numbers first. "Grind two clicks finer: your last three Yirgacheffes ran 30 s fast," not "Perfect your brew!" No exclamation marks, no coffee puns.

06

Calm means restraint in motion too. One 180 ms ease for state changes, no bounce, no parallax; the only thing allowed to animate continuously is the brew timer.

Never

  • Brown-on-brown: roast surfaces carrying roast text, or a full-bleed brown wash.
  • Gradients, glass blur, or drop shadows heavier than a 1 px hairline plus a soft y-offset.
  • Emoji or text glyphs as graphics: every star, arrow and mark is an inline SVG.
  • Latte-art hero photography, hand-drawn scenes, or a hand-drawn, photographic or emoji-style mascot. The mascot is geometric and stroke-consistent with the icon system, and it obeys the behaviour rules in 05: calm by default, expressive only on events, never looping.
  • A dark theme, an alternate palette, or any colour that is not one of the ten tokens.
  • Placeholder coffee: no lorem ipsum, no invented cupping scores, no fake origins.
  • Reading copy below 15 px, or a tap target under 44 px.
  • Two primary buttons for the same action in one viewport.
